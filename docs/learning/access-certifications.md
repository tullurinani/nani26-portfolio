# Access certifications and reviews

## What a certification is
An access certification is a periodic or event-driven review in which an authorised reviewer decides whether existing access is still appropriate. It is a detective governance control that helps identify access to retain, remove or investigate.

## Common review scopes
- **User access review:** review a person's access across applications.
- **Entitlement review:** review who has a particular permission or group.
- **Application owner review:** review access associated with an application.
- **Role review:** review role membership or role definitions.

Names and available campaign types vary by IGA product and configuration.

## A reliable review process
1. Define the scope, population and review period.
2. Confirm reviewer ownership and delegation rules.
3. Provide enough context for an informed decision.
4. Track approve, revoke and exception decisions.
5. Remediate revoked access in the target application.
6. Verify remediation and retain audit evidence.
7. Escalate overdue or disputed decisions.

## Common pitfalls
- Incomplete account correlation.
- Reviewers who do not understand the entitlement.
- Excessive review volume without useful context.
- Decisions recorded without downstream remediation.
- Exceptions without owners, expiry dates or justification.

## Practice exercise
Create a sample review with five fictional users and a small set of entitlements. Mark which access should be retained or revoked and explain the business reason for each decision.

## Interview checks
- What is the difference between an access request and a certification?
- Why does a revoke decision need provisioning and verification?
- What evidence should an auditor be able to retrieve?
